HUMAN DECISION

The scheduler selects the operational response.

The optimisation model can identify plans that satisfy the constraints represented in the model. The scheduler still decides which plan is operationally acceptable.

01 — EXAMPLE BASELINE

Vessel B is planned on Berth 2 from 10:00–14:00.

Vessel D is planned on the same berth from 15:00. This is the simplified baseline used only for this demonstration.

02 — EXAMPLE DISRUPTION

Vessel B is now expected four hours late.

If its four-hour service duration is unchanged, its berth use moves to approximately 14:00–18:00.

03 — WHY RECOVERY IS NEEDED

The delayed plan overlaps Vessel D.

Vessel B would still occupy Berth 2 when Vessel D is due to use it at 15:00. The scheduler therefore needs a revised plan.

04 — CANDIDATE RECOVERY ACTIONS

For this example, there are several sensible ways to respond.

Hold B until D clears, prioritise B and move D later, or test whether B can move to Berth 1 after Vessel C.

05 — OPTIMISATION ROLE

PortOptimiser tests the recovery combinations against the modelled constraints.

The calculation uses the current berth plan, the four-hour delay, berth and resource restrictions, and the recovery objective defined for the terminal.

06 — CONVERSATIONAL WHAT-IF

The scheduler can ask a specific operational question.

For example: can Vessel B use Berth 1 after Vessel C finishes? The conversational interface passes that scenario to the optimisation model; it does not decide feasibility itself.

07 — EXAMPLE COMPARISON

Compare the operational consequence of each candidate.

The useful question is not simply which option is “best”, but what changes for Vessel B, Vessel D, berth use and resource coordination under each recovery action.

All timings and candidate actions shown here are illustrative. A partner PoC would use the terminal’s actual layout, constraints and recovery priorities.
08 — HUMAN DECISION

The model supports the decision; the scheduler makes it.

A candidate can satisfy the constraints represented in the model and still require operational judgement. The scheduler remains responsible for the final recovery decision.
